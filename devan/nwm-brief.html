Veteran Recruiting at Northwestern Mutual: A Data-Driven Case

Prepared for Devan Jewell, Director of Recruiting

The Business Problem

Northwestern Mutual, like every major financial services firm, faces a dual talent crisis. The pipeline isn't replacing advisors fast enough, and the ones entering the pipeline aren't surviving. The question isn't whether NWM needs new talent. The question is: which talent survives?

The Veteran Thesis

Military veterans bring specific, mechanism-level advantages that directly address the number one reason rookies fail: inability to endure the prospecting grind.

Trained tolerance for sustained discomfort
Prospecting requires 2-3 years of rejection before the practice builds momentum
Mission discipline
Following NWM's training system rather than freelancing
Accountability culture
Fiduciary mindset — personal responsibility for client outcomes
Contingency planning
Financial planning is scenario-based by nature
Trust-building under pressure
Clients share fears, family situations, retirement anxieties — veterans know how to hold that space

Edward Jones consumer research found: 77% of individual investors associate military experience with discipline, 73% with goal-orientation, and 72% with integrity.

First Command Financial Services has operated with 75% veteran/military-spouse advisors since 1958 — managing $46.48B in client assets. The model has been proven for nearly seven decades.

The Opportunity

No firm has published comparative data showing veteran advisors outperform non-veteran peers. The first firm to build and publish this data owns the narrative.

The Data Gap — And Why It Matters Strategic Priority

No published study — from any firm or academic institution — compares veteran vs. non-veteran financial advisor performance on any metric: retention/attrition rates, AUM growth, client satisfaction, first-year survival rate, or time to profitability.

This is the single most valuable data NWM could collect.

What NWM Could Track Starting Now

Five metrics that would create a first-mover advantage in veteran recruiting data

Veteran vs. Non-Veteran 1-Year Retention
Directly tests the thesis that veterans survive the early grind at higher rates.
How to collect: Tag veteran status in recruiting pipeline; track at 6, 12, 18, 24 months.
Time to First 50 Clients
Measures prospecting effectiveness — the core skill that determines advisor survival.
How to collect: Already tracked in training metrics; break out by veteran status.
Client Retention by Advisor Background
Measures relationship quality — do clients of veteran advisors stay longer?
How to collect: Cross-reference advisor background with client churn data.
System Adherence Scores
Tests the "coachability" thesis — do veterans follow the NWM playbook more consistently?
How to collect: If NWM tracks training compliance, break out by background.
Source of First 100 Clients
Reveals whether veterans prospect differently — and whether that difference produces better outcomes.
How to collect: Survey or intake data during training reviews.

The payoff: If the data shows what the anecdotal evidence suggests — that veterans retain longer, build more loyal client bases, and follow the system more consistently — NWM has a recruiting story no competitor can match. This becomes a national recruiting campaign, not just a local initiative.

The Translation Problem

A 2024 peer-reviewed study (FIMT-RC, PMC) found that veterans' number one interview weakness is ineffective translation of military technical skills into civilian language. Not lack of competence — lack of translation.

The same study found that veterans who received interview training were significantly more likely to find jobs and to advance.

Implication for NWM: If candidates are washing out at the interview stage because they can't translate their experience — and that translation gap is fixable — then NWM is losing qualified candidates to a solvable problem.

Recommended: Candidate Prep Framework

A structured interview prep tool — the Veteran-to-Advisor Narrative Framework — maps four military archetypes to the NWM advisor value proposition with specific language translations. This can be used:

  1. Pre-interview: Send to candidates before they come in, so they arrive prepared to talk about their experience in terms NWM interviewers understand
  2. During intake conversations: Use as a framework for helping candidates see themselves in the advisor role
  3. In SkillBridge and TAC recruiting: Hand to potential candidates at events as a tangible takeaway
  4. As LinkedIn content: Share the framework publicly to attract candidates who self-select

The Retention Opportunity

From industry research and NWM's own experience, new advisor washout is driven by four primary failure modes:

#1
Not buying into the system
Thinking they can do it their own way instead of following the proven training playbook.
#2
Financial unpreparedness
Not having enough runway for variable income during the ramp-up period.
#3
Emotional/mental unpreparedness
Underestimating the difficulty of building a practice from scratch.
#4
Isolation
Not engaging with the office community and support network — going it alone.

Veterans should have structural advantages on #1 (system adherence), #2 (military pension/benefits for retirees), and #3 (resilience training). But #4 — isolation — is a real risk, especially for veterans transitioning from a deeply communal military culture.

Key Insight

The highest-leverage retention intervention for veteran advisors may be community and mentorship — ensuring they're connected, coming into the office, and paired with someone who's been through the hard first year.

The Hampton Roads Advantage

Norfolk is home to the world's largest Navy base. The local talent pipeline is uniquely favorable:

  • Constant flow of transitioning service members (both retiring and separating)
  • Active Transition Assistance Center on base
  • SkillBridge fair access through Hampton Roads Veteran Employment Center
  • Devan's personal credibility: 25 years Navy, both enlisted and officer, can speak to every pay grade

This office has the potential to become NWM's proof-of-concept for a national veteran recruiting strategy. If the data is tracked and the results are strong, the model can scale across every NWM office near a major military installation.
